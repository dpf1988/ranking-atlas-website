Key takeaways

  • Luxembourg leads 2024 at $135,321, the highest GDP per capita of any economy, a position it has held for most of the past four decades.
  • Six of 1960's top ten economies remain in the top ten in 2024: the United States, Switzerland, Luxembourg, Norway, Denmark, and Qatar.
  • Singapore recorded the largest climb into the top ten, rising from 32nd in 1960 to 6th in 2024.
  • Ireland gained nine places since 2000, the largest rise inside the current top 20, reaching 2nd at $106,059.
  • The global median rose from $459 to $13,140 between 1960 and 2024 in current US dollars; part of that increase reflects dollar inflation rather than real growth.

Six of 1960's top ten economies still rank in the top ten

Rank position, 1960 → 2024 · top 12 economies of 2024 · 1960 axis breaks below rank 16

The top of the GDP per capita table is unusually stable. The United States, which ranked 1st in 1960, sits 5th in 2024; Switzerland moved one place, from 2nd to 3rd; Luxembourg held inside the top three across the entire period. Where movement occurs, it concentrates in a small number of economies: petroleum exporters that entered high and slipped, and late industrialisers that entered low and climbed.

Singapore's rise from 32nd to 6th is the largest climb into the top ten

Singapore entered the panel in 1960 ranked 32nd, with a GDP per capita below $500 in current US dollars. By 2024 it ranks 6th at $84,734, the only economy outside 1960's top twenty to reach the current top ten. Ireland shows the second-largest sustained rise among advanced economies, moving from 14th in 1960 to 2nd in 2024, with most of the gain concentrated after 1995.

Movement in the opposite direction is dominated by petroleum exporters. Qatar, ranked 5th in 1960, sits 7th in 2024 after peaking and retreating with oil prices; Kuwait and Brunei show the two largest ten-year declines in the current top 40, falling 12 and 9 places respectively since 2014.

Frequently asked questions

Which country has the highest GDP per capita in 2024?

Luxembourg has the highest GDP per capita in 2024 at $135,321 in current US dollars, according to World Bank data. It has held that position for most of the past four decades. Ireland ranks 2nd at $106,059 and Switzerland 3rd at $99,995.

Which country has climbed the rankings most since 1960?

Among 2024's top ten, Singapore recorded the largest climb, rising from 32nd in 1960 to 6th in 2024. Across the full panel, several economies outside the top ten show larger absolute movements; the downloadable CSV includes the complete rank history.

How much has global GDP per capita grown since 1960?

The global median GDP per capita rose from $459 in 1960 to $13,140 in 2024 in current US dollars. Part of that increase reflects US-dollar inflation rather than real growth; constant-dollar series are available in the World Bank source data.

Where does this data come from?

All figures are drawn from the World Bank World Development Indicators, indicator NY.GDP.PCAP.CD, retrieved July 2026. Rankings are computed by Ranking Atlas across all 46 reporting economies for each year.

Methodology & Fair Use

Figures are GDP per capita in current US dollars as published in the World Bank World Development Indicators (indicator NY.GDP.PCAP.CD), retrieved July 2026. Rankings are computed across all 46 reporting economies for each year; economies with no reported value in a given year are excluded from that year's ranking. Because values are nominal, changes over time reflect both real growth and US-dollar inflation; within-year rankings are unaffected. Trajectory sparklines index each economy's series to its own 1960–2024 range and are not comparable in level across rows.
