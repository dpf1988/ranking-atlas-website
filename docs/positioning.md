# Ranking Atlas: positioning v1.1

*6 September 2026. Derived from the Eyelit engagement, the Jolly SEO arrangement, six months of unsold campaign inventory, and the site's own search and citation data. v1.1 incorporates the review pass and the earned-media correction.*

## The decision

Ranking Atlas sells a managed citation acquisition service to B2B software companies that want to be more visible in AI search. We start from the buyer questions that matter, identify the third-party sources influencing those answers, acquire relevant inclusion, earn coverage in the media that carries lasting authority, build the supporting onsite assets, and measure every phase against the original prompt and competitor baseline.

Citation acquisition has two layers: direct inclusion and earned authority. Every phase is designed to address both, with the balance determined by the citation landscape. The principle is mandatory; the allocation is not.

- **Direct inclusion.** Entering the sources today's answers already cite: comparison and top-10 pages, directories that carry weight, community and trade references. Fast, measurable within a phase, where most early movement comes from. This is an arbitrage on current engine behaviour and it will erode as engines filter extraction-optimised sources.
- **Earned authority.** Coverage in genuine editorial and trade media, earned with material a journalist would run: original data, a defensible finding, a story that cannot be written without citing the client. Slower, compounds, and survives filter changes because it is the same trust signal search has always rewarded and the engines retrieve through search. Producing that material is the capability the GEO shops do not have.

AI visibility is the outcome. Citation acquisition is the speciality. Digital PR, content and research are mechanisms. Measurement is the proof. Digital PR is not advertised or sold as a standalone product; buyers meet it inside a phase as how the client gets cited in places that last.

White-label per-link work for agencies continues as an unadvertised supply line.

Vocabulary rule: "citation acquisition" externally; "citation insertion" is internal shorthand for the direct-inclusion tactic and never appears in sales or site copy, because it sounds like link placement.

## Differentiator

We don't start by selling placements. We start from the sources already influencing the answers you want to win, enter them, and earn the coverage that will shape the next ones.

## What Eyelit actually bought

The enquiry, verbatim in substance: a growth marketer who owned AEO, GEO and SEO, already had a prompt list of twenty "best MES for X" and "top 10 APS vendors for Y" queries, already tracked cited domains in a HubSpot tool, already ran onsite comparison content and directory listings. He asked for three things:

1. Offsite placements in sources relevant to manufacturing software.
2. Content built with data Ranking Atlas could source itself, so his internal team stayed out of it.
3. A second opinion on the top-10 strategy.

The sale was made by running a baseline before the first call.

Phase 1 delivered: full audit, baseline across the prompt matrix, competitor analysis, citation landscape analysis (which domains the engines cite, classified by who owns them and whether they can be entered), placement verification with Majestic and Peec, and a vendor comparison page.

Phase 2 scope: per-prompt citation insertion, offsite placement strategy and execution, onsite assets built to the citation plan.

That is the product. It is not a campaign and it is not a retainer. It is a diagnosis followed by phases of intervention against a measured baseline.

## Who we sell to

Primary: B2B software and technology companies where AI answers already name vendors. Manufacturing software, cybersecurity, fintech infrastructure, cloud and data infrastructure, developer tools, privacy and compliance software, enterprise and specialist vertical SaaS.

Secondary, later: B2B technology-enabled companies and specialist B2B services where the same citation and authority mechanics apply.

B2B software is the primary ICP, not the market definition. What makes a company a prospect is the qualification below; software is where it holds most often. The hero does not say "for B2B software companies"; the ICP line directly beneath it does.

The buyer is the person who owns search and AI visibility in-house: a growth or demand marketer, a head of marketing, or a founder at a smaller company. They have usually been asked "why aren't we in ChatGPT" and have usually started on it themselves. They need offsite capability they don't have, content produced without internal politics, and proof they can show upward.

Qualification is four criteria, all required: commercial consideration queries exist in the category; AI answers already name competitors; the company cares about that visibility and has budget for it; there are identifiable external sources Ranking Atlas can influence. Revenue ($3M to $50M as a rough proxy for budget) stays an internal heuristic, not a rule.

The ideal customer has already started: they have the prompt list, they track competitors, they have fixed the obvious onsite issues, and they cannot build the external citation layer internally. That is a feature of the ICP, not a limitation. They are not hiring us to explain GEO; they are hiring us to execute the part they have identified as necessary.

Not: consumer, ecommerce, local services, affiliates, pre-revenue startups, categories where AI answers don't recommend vendors, anyone asking for a number of backlinks, anyone asking for guaranteed rankings.

## The offer ladder

**01. AI Visibility Breakdown. Free.**
Website plus up to three competitors, 10 to 20 representative buyer prompts across ChatGPT and Perplexity, current presence, competitor presence, the obvious source patterns, three to five high-level opportunities, and a short video walkthrough. It answers "is there an opportunity here and can Ranking Atlas attack it". It does not hand over the citation plan. This is what closed Eyelit and it is the only door.

**02. Citation Landscape. £3,500 to £4,500. Two to three weeks.**
Full prompt matrix baseline, competitor share, every cited domain classified (competitor-owned, directory or paid, editorial, community, capturable), placement verification, and a prioritised plan stating which sources can be entered, which can be earned, and how. Includes one onsite asset built to the plan. Eyelit paid $4,500 for this; do not price it as a £2,000 audit.

**03. Citation Acquisition Phase. £4,500 to £6,000 service fee, plus third-party media and placement costs where applicable. Four to six weeks.**
Execution against the landscape across both layers, weighted by what the landscape shows: direct inclusion in the sources the engines already cite, and earned coverage in editorial and trade media using material Ranking Atlas produces, with onsite assets built from data Ranking Atlas sources itself. Third-party costs are approved in advance, disclosed, selected on expected value, treated as client campaign spend, and never disguised as earned editorial. We do not sell a fixed number of links because the right source costs different amounts. Every phase ends with a re-measurement against baseline.

**04. Further phases. Same economics.**
Next query territory, next source set, next vertical.

**05. AI Visibility Monitoring. £750 to £1,250 per month.**
Monthly prompt runs, competitor share, alerts when a cited source changes. Offered after meaningful intervention, not as a lead product.

**White-label placements. Private pricing.** Per-link supply for agencies. Never on the site.

## Homepage capability section (digital PR, visible but not the product name)

Digital PR that earns its way into AI search. AI does not just need to find your website. It needs independent sources that establish who you are, what you do and why you belong in the category. We use original research, expert commentary, trade coverage and editorial PR to build that external authority, alongside direct inclusion in the sources already influencing today's answers.

## Internal rule

Never a placement marketplace. The client buys a citation acquisition strategy against a defined query set, not a count of links. The source landscape decides the tactic: a paid trade placement if it is the right source, a free contributed piece if that is better, a study if it opens fifteen legitimate opportunities, entry into one comparison page if that page carries 40% of the category's citations.
